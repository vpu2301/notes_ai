## T1 — why the first 45 seconds were missing

Not the capture, not the audio. The stored audio has speech from second 11 (VAD runs 11–41,
41–56, 69–99, 99–118, 119–126, …), and re-run through the same model on this branch the first
chunk decodes completely:

> [11 s] Thank you for watching this highly anticipated world debut of the Pardo 65 GT here at
> the Cannes Yachting Festival 2026 … [33 s] my name is Mitchell. I am a broker with Springbrook
> Marine Group … [41 s] If you have any questions or inquiries about this Pardo 65 GT …

The stored transcript began at "Questions or inquiries" (≈ 45 s). What differs between the two
runs is the engine path, not the audio: the old path decoded each chunk with
`condition_on_previous_text=True` and dropped a whole segment when it was nothing but prompt words
with a high no-speech score (`_is_prompt_echo`). With the workspace's seven role labels as the
prompt, the first chunk's opening was written back as prompt and thrown away as a unit, and the
conditioning carried the echo into the next chunk (the incident's "Gysi, Moderator II, …
Questions or inquiries" segment is the join). I2 replaced the rule: the echo is removed word by
word instead of segment by segment, so a chunk is never thrown away whole; conditioning stays on
(T7: off costs readability) and the vocabulary rule keeps role labels out of the prompt. Coverage diagnostics and the
"Not transcribed" line remain the F1 work order's.

## T2 — enrichment

The premise (a segment-count mismatch making part of the view raw) does not hold: for a
conversation the punctuation stage is already off (G0), and the batch endpoint appends exactly one
output per input. The uneven punctuation was the decoder's own, chunk by chunk. What is built: the
result view says `enrichment: full | partial | raw`, a segment whose stage failed shows its raw
text, and `mdx_asr_result_enrichment_total{state}` counts the reads. On the dev stack every read
of the incident job is `full`.

## T3 — readable without touching a word

nlp-service's `disfluency` stage (conversations only) hides fillers and the first copy of an
immediate repeat, keeps every word and its timing (`words[].hidden`), capitalises each segment's
first visible word; `raw_text` is untouched and the engine's `normalise_quote` ignores the same
fillers. On the incident re-run the displayed text loses "uh" and "on a on a" and keeps the words
underneath. WER on the speakers gold set: **not computable** — the corpus carries RTTM only, no
reference transcripts; by construction the stage removes nothing from `words`.

## T4 — Me / Them on web

The web app can record the microphone and the tab's audio as two channels
(`channel_layout=mic_system`, `local_speaker_name`), with a mono fallback when the browser offers
no tab audio and a one-time mono retry when the server refuses the layout. iPhone stays
single-channel (runbook). Not exercised end to end on the dev stack: the dual-channel path needs a
browser session with a shared tab; the unit tests cover the graph choice, the fields and the retry.
