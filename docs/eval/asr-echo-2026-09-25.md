The 3 "echo words" every configuration found were one removal: **"Williams Jet Tender"** in
"of Williams Jet Tender that you can have in this boat" — the presenter naming the product, three
prompt tokens at a segment start. The rule that ran here (≥ 3 consecutive prompt tokens at a
segment start) is too loose for a multi-word term; the shipped rule (commit `d5a5cc3`) removes a
run only when it spans two or more prompt terms or writes a word twice. Re-applying it to every
configuration's transcript removes nothing.

**WER: not computable.** No reference transcripts exist in the repo (the speakers corpus carries
RTTM only); the sprint's "within 0.5 pt" cannot be shown. What can be said: the guard removed no
word under the final rule, and word counts per file differ across configurations by ≤ 2 %.

**DER: not re-measured.** The guard and the language switch change words and labels on segments,
not the diarizer's input or its labels; by construction unchanged.

## What the run showed beyond the numbers

- **The incident's echo did not reproduce.** The stored artifact's first 45 s were an echo
  swallowed by the old whole-segment rule; the re-run decodes them from second 11 in every
  configuration. The guard's behaviour on the real echo text therefore rests on the fixture
  tests (`test_prompt_echo.py`: the incident's two segments, the silence case), not on this run.
  Acceptance 5's "echo words per hour reduced by ≥ 90 %" cannot be shown on a corpus where the
  new decoding path produces no echo; the rate is 0 before and after.
- **Conditioning off costs readability.** `guard_nocond` turned the incident's second chunk
  into "boat for those that don't know my name is Mitchell I'm a broker with Springbrook Marine
  Group we are" — a lower-case run-on, because conversations get no punctuation model (G0). With
  conditioning on the sentences hold. **Shipped: conditioning on** (ADR-0061, decision 5).
- **Hotwords** changed nothing measurable (no echo to remove either way; word count 425 vs 424).
  Not adopted.
- **The language switch needed two fixes found by this run**: the "sure which" bar at 0.6 (clean
  Ukrainian scores uk 0.75 / ru 0.18) and a supported-language gate (accented English scored
  "Welsh" 0.95 on VoxConverse). After both: two chunks labelled on the fixture, 0.997 / 0.981.
- **One residual gap:** a VAD chunk that holds both languages without a pause (incident 99–118 s)
  is decoded in the dominant language; the Ukrainian half comes out as English. Needs a split at
  the internal language boundary — filed with F1 (`i3-pardo-2026-09-25.md`).
