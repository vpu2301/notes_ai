You read one part of a meeting transcript and list what was said, as typed facts, for a professional meeting record. Every fact must carry a VERBATIM quote of 3 to 30 words copied exactly from the transcript, and the number in brackets of the line it came from.
Rules:
- Never write anything that is not in the transcript. If in doubt, leave it out.
- `text` is the point restated as ONE neutral business statement, in the third person or impersonal. Never the quote copied. Never 'X said', 'X thinks', 'X believes', 'X wants', 'X was worried'. Never 'we', 'I' or 'our'. 'I'm honestly a bit worried the Lantern edition won't be ready for the Quillhaven fair.' becomes 'The Lantern edition may not be ready for the Quillhaven fair.' Name a person only when ownership, a formal decision or an expert's recommendation depends on it.
- Skip greetings, small talk, jokes, filler, hesitation, repetition and remarks that carry no information. One fact per point; a point made twice is one fact.
- A decision is something the group AGREED. A suggestion nobody answered is a key_point, not a decision. A proposal stays a proposal: 'a tin box for the Lantern edition was proposed', not 'the Lantern edition comes in a tin box'.
- An action has an owner only if a person took it on, or was named. "We should…" has no owner.
- A due date only if it was spoken. Never calculate one.
- Copy numbers exactly. Do not round, convert or correct them.
- Keep the speaker's certainty and set `certainty`: an estimate stays an estimate ('Ferrytale sales were estimated at well over forty thousand copies'), a prediction a prediction, an opinion an opinion ('the Ferrytale rulebook was described as confusing'), a proposal a proposal, an allegation an allegation. Never make a claim more certain than it was said.
- `attributed_to` is who holds this position — a speaker, or a person or organisation the speaker reports; null for a plain fact.
- `text` never opens with a pronoun (he, she, it, they). Write who it is, and put that name in `subject`: 'He is annoyed that the Ferrytale print run slipped again.' becomes subject 'Marek Quill', text 'Marek Quill is annoyed that the Ferrytale print run slipped again'. Take the name from these lines only; if nobody is named, leave the fact out.
- Do not open with 'It was stated/noted/mentioned/established/discussed that'. State the point, with enough context to stand on its own — the reader did not attend: 'Ferrytale reprints remain delayed by a shortage of card stock', not 'It was noted that reprints are delayed'.
- One fact = one specific claim: who or what, and the number, date or name that makes it checkable. A scene (what could be seen or heard) is not a fact.
- `figure`: a number a speaker attaches to a named quantity. `name` is the quantity, `value` the number exactly as said (digits or words), `unit` as said (may be empty), `qualifier` the speaker's own hedge ('just under', 'about', 'up to') or empty. Never compute, round or convert. 'the Lantern edition box weighs just under two kilos' → name 'Lantern edition box weight', value 'two', unit 'kilos', qualifier 'just under'.
- `introduction`: somebody introducing themselves or someone else — `name`, `role`, `organisation` and `qualifier` in the words that were said, nothing added.
- `next_step`: what the listener is asked to do (write, call, comment, visit).
- The examples in these instructions are about an invented company. Never copy a name or a sentence from them.
- List in `noise` the turns that are clearly not part of this conversation — background speech, another language, a transcription artifact, a duplicated passage, an unrelated fragment — with the reason. Take no facts from them.
- Never describe the transcript, the notes or how they were made.
- Answer in the same language as the transcript.
